Safety

Test smoke alarms

What to do

  • Press and hold the test button.
  • Confirm the alarm sounds strongly.
  • Check each bedroom, hallway, and main living area.
If it does not test correctly after a fresh battery, replace the alarm or have it inspected.
Test carbon monoxide alarms
  • Press the test button.
  • Check the unit's expiration or replacement date.
  • Make sure it is not blocked.
If a CO alarm sounds unexpectedly, leave the area and treat it as a real warning until the cause is identified.
Test GFCI outlets
  • Press TEST.
  • The outlet should shut off.
  • Press RESET to restore power.
If it will not trip or reset, stop using it and have the circuit inspected.

Heating & Cooling

Inspect HVAC filter
  • Turn the system off.
  • Remove the old filter.
  • Confirm the replacement size.
  • Install it with the airflow arrow pointing toward the blower.
A clean filter helps airflow and can reduce strain on the system.
Inspect outdoor AC unit
  • Clear leaves, grass, and debris around the condenser.
  • Check for abnormal vibration or visible damage.
Disconnect electrical power before opening panels or working near fan blades.

Water & Plumbing

Check under sinks for leaks

If you find water

  • Dry the area completely.
  • Run the faucet and watch the supply lines and drain.
  • Check fittings for dripping.
  • Use the local shutoff valves if needed.
Call a plumber if the leak is inside a wall, a shutoff valve will not close, or you cannot identify the source.
Check toilets for leaks or running
  • Check the flapper and chain inside the tank.
  • Check the fill valve and water level.
  • If water appears at the base during flushing, stop using the toilet until the seal is checked.
Inspect water heater
  • Look for water underneath the tank.
  • Inspect visible piping for corrosion.
  • Listen for unusual popping or banging.
A tank leaking from the body itself usually needs professional evaluation and often replacement.

Exterior & Appliances

Inspect foundation for cracks or damage

What to look for

  • New cracks in poured concrete, block, or brick foundation walls.
  • Cracks that appear wider than before or continue to grow.
  • Horizontal cracks, stair-step cracks in masonry, or sections that look bowed or displaced.
  • Water staining, dampness, efflorescence, or active seepage near the crack.
  • Doors or windows that suddenly stick, floors that feel newly uneven, or gaps opening around trim.

What you can do

  • Take a clear photo and date it so you can compare the crack over time.
  • Mark the ends lightly with a pencil to see whether the crack extends.
  • Check nearby grading, gutters, and downspouts to make sure water is directed away from the home.
Do not simply cover a large or changing crack with patch material before determining why it formed.
Have a qualified foundation professional or structural engineer evaluate cracks that are large, widening, horizontal, stair-step shaped, accompanied by wall movement, or associated with significant water intrusion or sudden changes inside the home.
Inspect roof shingles

What to look for

  • Missing, lifted, curled, cracked, or visibly damaged shingles.
  • Bare spots where protective granules appear to be worn away.
  • Loose flashing around chimneys, vents, skylights, or roof edges.
  • Shingle pieces or excessive granules collecting in gutters or on the ground.
  • New water stains on ceilings or in the attic after rain.

Safe inspection

  • Do the initial inspection from the ground using binoculars or your phone camera zoom.
  • Check again after strong wind, hail, or severe storms.
  • Document suspicious areas with photos so you can compare changes over time.
Avoid walking on the roof unless you are trained and equipped to do so. Wet, steep, damaged, or high roofs can be especially dangerous.
Contact a qualified roofer if you see missing shingles, widespread curling or cracking, damaged flashing, sagging areas, active leaks, or significant storm damage.
Inspect trees and overhanging limbs

What to look for

  • Dead, cracked, hanging, or partially broken limbs.
  • Branches rubbing against the roof, siding, gutters, windows, or chimney.
  • Limbs hanging over the house, driveway, vehicles, play areas, or walkways.
  • Branches extending over a neighboring property.
  • Large limbs with visible splits, cavities, decay, fungus, or weak attachment points.
  • Trees or limbs leaning toward structures more than they previously did.

What you can do

  • Photograph questionable limbs and check them again after storms or high winds.
  • Keep small branches from continuously rubbing against shingles, siding, or gutters.
  • Before trimming branches that extend over a neighbor's property, coordinate with the neighbor and follow applicable local rules.
Do not cut large, suspended, storm-damaged, or heavily loaded limbs yourself. Never trim branches near electrical service lines or utility wires.
Contact a qualified arborist for large limbs, high branches, storm damage, significant decay, unstable trees, or branches that could strike a structure. Contact the utility provider for vegetation near power lines.
Inspect gutters and downspouts
  • Look for leaves and debris.
  • Make sure downspouts are connected.
  • Confirm water exits away from the foundation.
Use appropriate ladder safety and avoid roof work in unsafe conditions.
Inspect dryer vent
  • Clean the lint screen.
  • Check that the exterior vent opens while running.
  • Look for lint buildup behind the dryer.
Restricted dryer vents can increase drying time and create a fire hazard.

Weekly Lawn Checklist

Your secondary task list for basic lawn upkeep.

0% complete
Mow the lawn
  • Mow when the grass is dry.
  • Avoid removing more than about one-third of the grass blade at one time.
  • Alternate mowing direction periodically.
  • Watch for scalping, uneven cutting, or clumping.
The ideal mowing height depends on your grass species and season.
Edge sidewalks and driveway
  • Trim growth along hard surfaces.
  • Keep the edge shallow enough to avoid damaging irrigation lines or cable.
Inspect for weeds
  • Walk the yard and identify new patches early.
  • Hand-pull isolated weeds when practical.
  • Avoid blanket chemical treatment unless you know the weed and product requirements.
Check soil moisture and watering
  • Look for wilt, footprints that remain visible, or dry soil.
  • Water deeply rather than using frequent shallow watering.
  • Adjust for rainfall and local restrictions.
Pick up sticks and debris
  • Clear sticks, toys, rocks, and other objects before mowing.
  • Inspect for storm damage or low branches.
Quick mower inspection
  • Check for loose hardware or obvious damage.
  • Check fuel, battery, or charge level as applicable.
  • Keep the deck clear of heavy buildup.
Disconnect the spark plug or power source before reaching under a mower deck.

Seasonal Maintenance

A quick set of tasks that change throughout the year.

0% complete

Spring

Clean gutters and verify drainage
  • Remove winter debris.
  • Confirm downspouts discharge away from the foundation.
  • Look for loose gutter sections.
Prepare AC for cooling season
  • Clear the outdoor condenser.
  • Install a clean HVAC filter.
  • Test cooling before the first hot spell.
Walk the exterior after winter
  • Check shingles, siding, foundation, trim, and trees for winter damage.

Summer

Inspect hoses, sprinklers, and outdoor faucets
  • Check for leaks and damaged fittings.
  • Make sure water is not pooling near the foundation.
Check trees after storms
  • Look for cracked, hanging, or roof-rubbing limbs.
  • Use an arborist for high or hazardous limbs.

Fall

Prepare heating system
  • Replace or inspect the filter.
  • Test heat before cold weather arrives.
  • Keep vents clear.
Clear leaves from gutters
  • Remove leaf buildup and check downspouts before freezing weather.
Winterize outdoor water lines
  • Disconnect hoses.
  • Drain or protect exposed outdoor plumbing as appropriate for your climate.

Winter

Watch for freeze-related plumbing problems
  • Check vulnerable plumbing during severe cold.
  • Know where the main water shutoff is located.
Check roof and gutters after winter storms
  • Look from the ground for damage, heavy ice, or loose materials.
